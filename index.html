02 / The people

Five perspectives.
One uncertain future.

An AGI scientist, an AI-risk theorist, an algorithmic accountability researcher, a technology journalist, and an entrepreneur share the documentary's conversation.

AGI research · AI safety

Shane Legg

Works at: Google DeepMind · Role: Co-founder and Chief AGI Scientist

Legg earned a PhD at the Dalle Molle Institute for Artificial Intelligence Research (IDSIA). At DeepMind, he helped advance research toward AGI, including deep reinforcement learning that lets systems learn from experience. He leads the technical AGI safety team and publicly advocates for taking existential risk seriously. His honors include appointment as a Commander of the Order of the British Empire and the Digital Masters Award for Excellence in Technology. Sources ↗

“I don't think anybody has a good estimate of this.”

— Shane Legg, on the chance of human extinction around human-level AI; full quotation and source ↗

AI alignment · Existential risk

Eliezer Yudkowsky

Works at: Machine Intelligence Research Institute · Role: Co-founder and longtime AI-risk theorist

Yudkowsky is a founding researcher at the Machine Intelligence Research Institute and is associated with the early development of AI alignment as a field. He founded the community blog LessWrong in 2009, wrote extensively about AI risk, and contributed chapters to academic collections including The Cambridge Handbook of Artificial Intelligence and Global Catastrophic Risks. The presentation describes him as deeply concerned about AI's future and notes his proposals for limiting the power of advanced AI. Sources ↗

AI outlook The presentation characterizes Yudkowsky as strongly worried about AI's potential catastrophic risks. No direct quotation from him was included in the submitted slides.

Algorithmic accountability · AI bias

Deborah Raji

Works at: UC Berkeley; affiliated with Mozilla · Role: Researcher focused on auditing and accountability

Raji researches the social impacts of AI and advocates for more transparent, accountable systems. Her work with the Algorithmic Justice League examined facial-recognition technology and its effects on marginalized communities. She has also served on advisory boards focused on AI governance, health AI, and civil rights and technology. The presentation describes her as concerned about AI risks and bias. Sources ↗

“If you feed that into an AI model, it adopts this world view and adapts its decisions based on those biases.”

— Deborah Raji, discussing representation and AI bias; source ↗

Technology journalism · AI and society

Nitasha Tiku

Works at: The Washington Post · Role: Technology culture reporter

Tiku studied English at Columbia University and journalism at New York University. She has covered Silicon Valley and technology culture for more than a decade, including work for WIRED, BuzzFeed News, The Verge, Valleywag, and New York Magazine. Her reporting examines how AI affects society and behavior, including generative AI, AI companions, relationships, and consumer psychology. The presentation describes her approach as skeptical and concerned. Source ↗

AI outlook The presentation characterizes Tiku as skeptical about AI's safety and social effects. No direct quotation from her was included in the submitted slides.

Innovation · Entrepreneurship

Peter Diamandis

Works at: XPRIZE and Singularity University · Role: Founder and chair

Diamandis founded and chairs XPRIZE, whose competitions have awarded more than $600 million and, according to the presentation, driven over $10 billion in research and development. He co-founded Singularity University and several ventures, holds degrees in molecular genetics and aerospace engineering as well as an MD from Harvard Medical School, and is a bestselling author. The presentation describes his view of AI as positive and focused on its potential to improve the future. Sources ↗

AI outlook The presentation characterizes Diamandis as optimistic about AI's potential. No direct quotation from him was included in the submitted slides.
