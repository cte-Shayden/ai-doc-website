The AI Doc

The People
Behind AI

The AI Doc: Or How I Became an Apocaloptimist is a documentary by Daniel Roher and Charlie Tyrell. It's about how people feel about where AI is headed in the next few years, and it basically asks one big question: should we be scared of AI or excited about it?

01 / 05

Apocaloptimist

Someone who thinks something really bad might happen, but still stays hopeful about the future.

The film asks things like, “Is it a good time to have a child?” and “Will AI bring a sort of doomsday upon us?” Source ↗

02 / Interviews

People in
The AI Doc

Here are five of the people who get interviewed in the documentary.

Google DeepMind

Shane Legg

Works at: Google DeepMind · Role: Co-founder and Chief AGI Scientist

Legg got his PhD at IDSIA and later helped build DeepMind, which works on artificial general intelligence (AI that can do a lot of different things, not just one). DeepMind used deep reinforcement learning, which lets an AI learn from experience, like figuring out how to play Atari games. Today Legg leads the technical AGI safety team. He's talked a lot about the risks of AI and has signed statements about AI safety. He's also won the CBE and the Digital Masters Award. Source ↗

“Eventually, I think human extinction will probably occur, and technology will likely play a part in this. But there's a big difference between this being within a year of something like human level AI, and within a million years. As for the former meaning...I don't know. Maybe 5%, maybe 50%. I don't think anybody has a good estimate of this.”

— Shane Legg, source ↗

Machine Intelligence Research Institute

Eliezer Yudkowsky

Works at: Machine Intelligence Research Institute · Role: Co-founder and longtime AI-risk theorist

Yudkowsky helped start the Machine Intelligence Research Institute and is one of the people behind the whole field of AI alignment (making sure AI actually does what humans want). He started LessWrong in 2009 and has written hundreds of articles about AI risks. He also wrote chapters in The Cambridge Handbook of Artificial Intelligence and Global Catastrophic Risks, and he's given a TED Talk. He's suggested some pretty intense ways to limit AI, like letting the government take out a rogue data center with an airstrike. According to our slides, he's extremely worried about AI. Source ↗

UC Berkeley

Deborah Raji

Works at: UC Berkeley · Role: Researcher in AI auditing and accountability

Raji studies bias in AI and how it affects real people. She's a Mozilla Foundation fellow, and she also worked with the Algorithmic Justice League on facial recognition and how it hurts marginalized communities. She's on advisory boards for AI governance, health AI, and civil rights, and she's been named to Forbes 30 Under 30, MIT Technology Review's 35 Under 35, and the TIME 100 in AI. Our slides say she's worried about the dangers of AI. Source ↗

“Let’s say I want examples of what healthy skin looks like. If you Google it now, you will see mostly light-skinned women,” says Raji. “You won’t see a man for pages, and you wouldn’t see a darker-skinned woman until you really scroll down. If you feed that into an AI model, it adopts this world view and adapts its decisions based on those biases.”

— Deborah Raji, source ↗

The Washington Post

Nitasha Tiku

Works at: The Washington Post · Role: Technology culture reporter

Tiku studied English at Columbia University and journalism at New York University. She's written for WIRED, BuzzFeed News, The Verge, Valleywag, and New York Magazine. Now she covers tech culture at The Washington Post, where she writes about how AI changes people's lives and society, including AI companions and relationships. Our slides say she's skeptical and concerned about AI. Source ↗

XPRIZE and Singularity University

Peter Diamandis

Works at: XPRIZE and Singularity University · Role: Founder and chair

Diamandis founded XPRIZE, whose contests have given out over $600 million in prizes and helped fund more than $10 billion in research and development. He also co-founded Singularity University, Longevity Exponential Adventures, and BOLD Adventures. He has degrees in molecular genetics and aerospace engineering, plus an MD from Harvard Medical School, and he's a bestselling author. Our slides say he's positive about AI. Sources ↗
